---

## 1. What Is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, systems, applications, and data from unauthorised access, attacks, damage, or disruption.

---

## 2. What Is a Threat?

A threat is something that has the potential to cause harm to a computer system, network, organisation, or user.

Examples include:

* Malware
* Phishing
* Ransomware
* Social engineering
* Unauthorised access

---

## 3. What Is a Vulnerability?

A vulnerability is a weakness in a system that could potentially be exploited by an attacker.

Examples include:

* Weak passwords
* Outdated software
* Misconfigured systems
* Unpatched security vulnerabilities

---

## 4. What Is Risk?

Risk is the possibility that a threat could take advantage of a vulnerability and cause harm.

A simple way to think about it is:

**Threat + Vulnerability = Potential Risk**

---

## 5. What Is an Attack?

A cyber attack occurs when someone attempts to compromise a system, network, account, application, or data.

Examples include:

* Phishing attacks
* Malware attacks
* Password attacks
* Denial-of-service attacks

---

## 6. What Is an Incident?

A cybersecurity incident is an event that threatens or compromises the security of information systems or data.

For example:

A company employee clicks on a malicious link and their account becomes compromised.

This could become a cybersecurity incident that needs to be investigated and contained.

---

## 7. The CIA Triad

One of the most important cybersecurity concepts is the **CIA Triad**.

### Confidentiality

Ensuring that information is only accessible to authorised people.

### Integrity

Ensuring that information remains accurate and has not been improperly changed.

### Availability

Ensuring that systems and information are available when authorised users need them.
